# Interview guide: GMP readiness and validation work (Mom Test)

**Purpose:** test the riskiest assumption from `docs/STRATEGY.md`: *non-cannabis EU-GMP manufacturers and ISO/IEC 17025
laboratories already pay outside firms for GMP-readiness and validation work, at prices that support our margins.*
**Never describe the offer.** Ask about their life and their past behaviour. 30 minutes, video or on site, one interviewer.

## Who qualifies (the screen)
Talk only to someone who **personally owned or approved** at least one of these in the last 24 months:
- a regulatory GMP inspection, or a GMP/ISO 17025 certification or re-certification;
- an analytical method validation or transfer, a stability programme, or a computerised-system (Annex 11) validation;
- an outsourced audit, gap assessment or consultancy engagement on quality.

Typical roles are Head of QA/QC, QP, Quality Director, Lab Manager and Technical Director, at a company of 20–300 staff.
**Not qualified:** friends, former colleagues who would protect your feelings, and anyone linked to your current employer (customers, suppliers, contract laboratories, business associates). The contract forbids contact with them, and they would bias the answers anyway.

## Opening (2 min)
"I'm researching how quality teams in [country] handle inspections and validation work. I'm not selling anything today.
Can I ask about what you've actually done in the last couple of years?"

## Core questions (broad to specific)
| # | Ask this | Not this (leading) | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Tell me about your last regulatory inspection or certification. When was it, and how did you prepare?" | "Are inspections stressful?" | Gets a real story, not an opinion |
| 2 | "What was the hardest part of that preparation? What went wrong?" | "Would faster preparation help?" | The pain in their words |
| 3 | "Who did the work: your people, or someone outside? Why that choice?" | "Do you use consultants?" | Make-vs-buy behaviour |
| 4 | "The last time you brought in an outside firm for audit, validation or readiness work: what was it, how was it priced, roughly how much, and how long did it take?" | "How much would you pay for…?" | **Money already spent. This decides the test.** |
| 5 | "How did you find and choose that firm? Who signed off on the spend?" | "Where would you look for help?" | The real buying path and the decision maker |
| 6 | "What did you think of the result? Would you hire them again? Why or why not?" | "Was it good value?" | What outsiders get wrong today |
| 7 | "What's coming in the next 12 months: an inspection, new products, a new site, the Annex 11 or Annex 22 changes? How are you planning for it?" | "Will you need help with Annex 22?" | A trigger, a deadline, a budget |
| 8 | "How do you handle documentation and CoA review today? Roughly how many hours a month go on it?" | "Would AI help your documents?" | The size of the recurring pain (the desk offer) |

### Follow-ups for specifics (use constantly)
- "When exactly was that? What did you do next?"
- "What did that cost you in money, days, or a delayed release?"
- "Can you show me / do you still have that document, quote or report?"

## Close (3 min): ask for a commitment, never a compliment
1. "Who else should I talk to who went through this recently?" (a referral costs them reputation, so it is a real signal)
2. Only at the end, and only if the pain was clearly strong: "If I could take on part of [their specific upcoming job], could we scope it? Would a paid diagnostic be worth discussing?" (**This is the pass-criterion question.** A deposit or a signed SOW counts; "interesting, send me something" doesn't.)

## Signals vs anti-signals
| Real signal (count it) | Anti-signal (ignore it) |
|---|---|
| A specific past story with a date, a name and a number | "In general, we usually…" |
| Money already paid to outsiders, with the amount named | "We would pay for something like that" |
| Emotion: "that inspection nearly killed us" | Compliments: "great idea" |
| A dated trigger ahead (inspection, launch, new site) | "Someday, maybe" |
| A referral, a meeting with the boss, a deposit, a SOW | "Send me a brochure" |

## Rules
- Shut up after each question. Let the silence work.
- No pitching, no correcting them, no explaining your solution.
- Write down **verbatim quotes** the same day, in `NOTES_TEMPLATE.md`.
- Judge after all 10, not after the second.
